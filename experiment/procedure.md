### Procedure

#### Materials Required
* Actively growing host cells (e.g., bacterial culture for bacteriophage study)
* Virus stock suspension
* Suitable growth medium
* Incubator at appropriate temperature
* Centrifuge
* Sterile pipettes and tubes
* Agar plates for plaque assay
* Water bath (if required for phage adsorption)

#### Step-by-Step Procedure

1. **Preparation of Host Cells**
   The host cells are grown to the logarithmic phase to ensure active metabolism and optimal susceptibility to viral infection.

2. **Infection of Host Cells**
   A measured volume of virus suspension is added to the host cell culture at a high multiplicity of infection (MOI). The mixture is allowed to stand for a short time (usually 5–10 minutes) to permit adsorption of the virus to host cells.

3. **Removal of Unadsorbed Virus**
   The culture is centrifuged to separate infected cells from free virus particles. The supernatant containing unadsorbed viruses is discarded, and the infected cells are resuspended in fresh medium.

4. **Incubation**
   The infected culture is incubated at the appropriate temperature to allow viral replication.

5. **Sampling at Regular Intervals**
   Samples are withdrawn at fixed time intervals (for example, every 5 or 10 minutes).
   * For determining intracellular virus, cells may be lysed before assay.
   * For extracellular virus, samples are directly assayed.

6. **Estimation of Viral Titer (Plaque Assay)**
   Each sample is serially diluted and mixed with fresh host cells in soft agar. The mixture is poured onto agar plates and incubated. After incubation, clear zones called plaques appear, each representing infection by a single virus particle. The number of plaques is counted and expressed as PFU/mL.

7. **Plotting the Growth Curve**
   The viral titer (\(\log \text{PFU/mL}\)) is plotted on the Y-axis against time on the X-axis. The graph shows the eclipse phase, latent phase, rise period, and plateau phase.
